Built for people who read the Greeks

Serious plumbing under a friendly surface

01

Collateralised before it rests

An offer locks the writer's maximum payout and a bid escrows the buyer's premium, both before they appear on the book. Nothing quoted here can fail to pay.

02

No house, no pool

Every option is written by another user who posts their own collateral. The protocol matches and settles; it never takes the other side of your trade and holds no position.

03

Every series stands alone

Collateral is held per series, so a loss in one market cannot reach another. There is no shared pot for a bad market to drain.

04

Oracle-settled, dispute-windowed

Expiry is settled at the Chainlink round that straddled it, not whatever the price is when someone gets around to settling, with a challenge window before funds move.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask before their first contract.

Can I lose more than I put in?

Not when you buy. A long call or long put costs you the premium and nothing else — there is no margin account to top up and no liquidation price to defend. Selling options is different: writing a contract means you take on the obligation, so those positions are collateralised and can lose more than the premium you collected. The app labels which is which before you sign.

What happens if I forget about a position?

Nothing bad. Contracts settle automatically against the oracle price at expiry. If you finish in the money the USDC is credited to your wallet without you doing anything; if you finish out of the money the contract expires and no further action is possible or needed.

Where does the liquidity come from?

A delta-hedged pool takes the other side of every trade, funded by depositors who are paid the premium flow. The pool re-hedges its net exposure on each fill rather than sitting naked short volatility, which is what lets it quote continuously instead of widening out the moment the market moves.

How is the premium calculated?

A Black-Scholes surface fitted to live implied volatility, adjusted for pool utilisation and the skew of existing open interest. The pricer at the top of this page runs the same core model, so what you see there is the shape of what you would actually pay.

Which assets can be listed?

Any asset with a qualifying oracle feed and enough depth to hedge against. Majors and commodities are listed by default; long-tail tokens can be listed permissionlessly once their feed clears the liquidity threshold, which is what keeps meme markets from being unhedgeable.

Hockeystick is live on Robinhood Chain mainnet and has not been audited. Nothing here is financial advice or an offer. Trading options risks real money: a buyer can lose the entire premium paid, and a writer’s collateral is locked until expiry and paid out to the holder if the option finishes in the money. Because every option is collateralised in full when it is written, no position can be liquidated — but unaudited smart contracts can contain defects that cost you everything in them, and settlement depends on Chainlink price feeds continuing to publish. Payouts on calls are capped at a per-market multiple of the strike, so a call is economically a call spread rather than uncapped upside. Derivatives trading is restricted or prohibited in many jurisdictions; it is your responsibility to know whether you may use this.
